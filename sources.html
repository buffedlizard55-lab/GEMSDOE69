Geology and geophysics: primary sources

  • Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229 — Analysis of Step-Overs / Relay Ramps

    Primary workshop proceedings paper from Stanford's geothermal engineering series. Its text supports the documented step-over inventory, typical elongated geometry, measured ramp-width range/mean, breaching-fault styles, higher-temperature hard-link/overlap observations, the Walker Lane domain contrast, the WNW extension orientation rationale, and use of gravity, magnetic, MT, lidar, and mapped fault data. The paper also cautions that high-resolution geological/fault data are needed and that geophysical layers alone are insufficient. These are source observations, not validation results from this model.

  • Faulds, Hinz & Coolbaugh (2010), OSTI — Structural investigations of Great Basin geothermal fields

    U.S. Department of Energy Office of Scientific and Technical Information record. It discusses Walker Lane dextral shear transfer, regional WNW extension, N–NNE normal faults, and favorable fault interaction zones. Use as regional context, not as proof for each candidate pixel.

  • USGS Publications Warehouse record 70271401 · USGS abstract page

    Both official pages are available. The Publications Warehouse cites Glen & Earney (2025); the USGS abstract page displays a December 1, 2024 page date but its citation block also gives 2025. The reported magnetic high is interpreted as a buried mafic dike, and springs occur at anomaly breaks/bends. This is a strong non-fault alternative for magnetic lineaments and supports recording lithologic/intrusive mimics. Use 2025 per the formal citation; flag the page-date discrepancy.

  • USGS GeoDAWN data release page · DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ

    Official USGS page describing the airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys, survey coverage, processing products, and coordinated lidar. It identifies the data release as CC0. This supports source provenance and layer interpretation, not the competition's protected template grid.

  • Great Basin Center for Geothermal Energy — INGENIOUS project

    University of Nevada, Reno center page listing the project and its associated data/publications, including the cited GDR DOI for the regional compilation. This is a project-source reference, not evidence that a local GDR download succeeded.

Potentially useful external data: checked, not used

  • USGS ScienceBase — Quaternary-fault slip and dilation tendency · DOI 10.5066/P9YL58W6

    The official USGS metadata lists downloadable shapefile/KMZ ZIPs for the Great Basin and INGENIOUS area, and says they include fault geometry and the stress data used to calculate tendencies. This is a concrete source to investigate if the competition package lacks stress orientation, but it is not a ready-to-use independent predictor: the features are tied to a Quaternary-fault catalogue and could leak or duplicate catalogue labels. No attachment was downloaded or inspected here; do not use precomputed fault tendencies unless its lineage and fold-local leakage behavior are proven clean.

  • User-provided GDR submission link 1391

    Fetched during this review; the endpoint returned “No submission found.” The INGENIOUS center page does cite a corresponding regional-dataset DOI, but the provided URL is not currently a verified usable download in this environment. Do not treat sibling-site file-size claims or mirrors as proof of availability.

  • USGS National Map 3DEP one-meter DEM collection · USGS download and format FAQ

    Official USGS metadata confirms a public-domain one-meter bare-earth DEM collection, with CONUS tiles in UTM; the FAQ confirms downloads are free, require no account, and are available as GeoTIFF/IMG through The National Map Downloader. This is a specific, obtainable source for a DEM-derived lidar-scarp layer. Exact tile coverage over the competition AOI, source dates, and compatibility with the official sample grid have not been checked; no tiles are present locally. The competition's own DEM-link CSV remains behind login.

  • USGS GeoDAWN data release · DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ

    The official metadata confirms public GeoDAWN magnetic and radiometric products and notes coordinated 3DEP lidar over a similar extent. The attached-file list reviewed here contains the magnetic/radiometric grids, flight paths, outlines, and reports; it does not establish that the 1 m DEM tiles needed for this model are included in this release.
