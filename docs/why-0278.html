3. Why removing dots raised the score — the metric algebra [official formula, model arithmetic]

The metric is the distance-weighted Tversky index with α = 0.2, β = 0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel (competition page). For a binary dot field with weighted true positives T, emitted mass S, self-credit M and hidden truth size |G|:

DTI = T / ( 0.2·(T + S − M) + 0.8·|G| )
adding one non-redundant dot with kernel credit k raises DTI  ⟺  k > 0.2·DTI

At 0.2778 the break-even is k > 0.056: a dot pays only if it lies within ~283 m of an uncovered hidden-fault pixel. Three consequences explain the whole ladder:

  1. Dotting beats solid ribbons. A dot on a 1-D trace covers ±2 px of it with kernel weights 1, ⅔, ⅓, so 2.8-px spacing keeps most of the coverage at a third of the mass. Solid → dotted (+0.0555, +0.0123) is that effect.
  2. Known faults are masked from scoring (organizer statement, forum thread 11516). A dot 100–200 m from a mapped trace earns credit only if a new fault runs within 300 m of it. Deleting those dots raised the score twice (+0.0108, +0.0070), i.e. their mean credit was below the 0.054–0.056 bar: new faults rarely run within 100–200 m of the published traces.
  3. The field underneath carries the credit. Every rung kept the h19-5 ranking; under the family's live-anchor inversion (|G| ≈ 12,226 px [model]) the champion's mean credit is ≈ 0.13–0.14 per dot, 2.4× the bar.

So 0.2778 is best read as near-minimal mass on a reasonably ranked wide field, not as proof that its field is the right geology.

4. Can it be beaten?

  • By more mass control: a little, but the cheap moves are spent and every remaining move re-uses the champion's own dots, which fails this protocol's uniqueness rule (> 70 % of dots within 3 px of a registry raster). Not available to a unique submission.
  • By better ranking (credit per dot): this is the only large lever. Raising mean credit per dot from ≈ 0.135 to ≈ 0.19 at the same mass moves the algebra to ≈ 0.36 [model]. The public board shows that much better fields exist: #1 0.4275, #9 0.3195 on 2026-10-12 (leaderboard; snapshot registry/leaderboard_snapshot_2026-10-12.json).
  • By this lane (step-over corridors): tested here. On the hide-and-recover holdout the corridor-restricted field ranks withheld faults worse than the same pipeline without corridor priors, so it is not a route past 0.2778 on current evidence. See evidence/e2_gate.json and the run card.
