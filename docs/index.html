What this lane is

Hidden faults are proposed from catalogue geometry, not pixel statistics: the tips of mapped fault strands form a graph; step-over tip pairs (spacing 0.1–15 km, along-strike separation ≤ 3.5W, sub-parallel strikes) emit relay-ramp corridors — an oblique connector plus en échelon strands — and, at dilatational steps under the Walker-Lane dextral prior, pull-apart rhombs. Corridors are scored by multi-scale horizontal-gradient and tilt evidence in gravity and magnetics (including magnetic lows), the 1 m lidar-scarp channel, and orientation consistency with WNW extension. Dots are placed by greedy marginal-DTI selection inside the top corridors (pre-registered propensity map S0=0.75; template allocator), ≥200 m from the mapped catalogue.

Geometry priors as cited in the standing prompt: Giddens & Faulds (2025, SGP-TR-229) inventoried >450 step-overs; ramps are typically 3–3.5× longer than wide (widths 0.1–14.6 km, mean 2.8 km); ~70% of higher-temperature step-overs are hard-linked; in the Walker Lane, hot systems sit more often in pull-aparts and displacement-transfer zones; N–NE faults are best oriented for dilation under WNW extension.
