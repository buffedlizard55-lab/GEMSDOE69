What this lane is

Hidden faults proposed from catalogue geometry: mapped strands → tip graph (3,887 strands, 383,827 tip pairs, 117,006 de-duplicated step-overs); each step-over (spacing 0.1–15 km, along-strike separation ≤3.5W) emits an oblique connector plus en échelon strands, or a pull-apart at dilatational steps. Corridors are scored by multi-scale horizontal-gradient and tilt evidence in gravity and magnetics (incl. magnetic lows), the lidar-scarp channel and N–NE orientation; dots are placed by the template's greedy marginal-DTI allocator, ≥200 m from mapped faults.

Verified this session in Giddens & Faulds (2025, SGP-TR-229): >450 step-overs; widths 0.1–14.6 km (mean 2.8 km); ramps typically 3–3.5× longer than wide; ~70% of >120 °C step-overs hard-linked; Walker Lane is the only domain where step-overs are not the most common favorable setting, hosts no step-over power plants, and has the narrowest ramps (mean 1.9 km). The prompt's wording that Walker Lane hot systems sit “more often in pull-aparts and displacement-transfer zones” is an inference, not a statement in the paper (IR-69-SRC-01).
