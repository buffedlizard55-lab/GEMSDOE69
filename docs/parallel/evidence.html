Relay-ramp and Walker Lane claims

Claim / specificationVerificationInterpretation and limitSource
The INGENIOUS study mapped more than 450 step-overs.Supported. The paper identifies 454 total step-overs in the study area.This is a regional inventory count, not a promise that every catalogue tip pair is a true step-over.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, results / figure caption.
Ramps are elongated, typically about three to three-and-a-half times longer than wide.Supported. The paper's overview describes this typical aspect ratio.Use as a geometric prior, not as a deterministic fault-generation formula; natural ramps vary with overlap, spacing, throw, mechanical stratigraphy, and linkage stage.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, section 3.
Measured ramp widths span 0.1–14.6 km; mean width is 2.8 km.Supported. About 70% of higher-temperature step-overs are hard-linked.Supported with definition. The paper reports about 70.4% (rounded to ~70%) for higher-temperature step-overs.Applies to the paper's classified sample of known higher-temperature systems, not all candidate ramps and not a calibrated probability for an individual gap.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, abstract / implications.
Breaching faults can be oblique connectors or en-échelon strands.Supported. The abstract states that minor faults breaching step-overs vary between oblique-slip connectors and en-échelon faults parallel to the main strands.These are candidate geometry families; do not assume both exist in every ramp.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, abstract.
Walker Lane higher-temperature settings favor pull-aparts / displacement transfer more than normal-fault step-overs.Supported as a domain contrast. The paper says Walker Lane is the domain where step-overs are not the most common favorable setting; it notes that in transtensional settings, higher-temperature systems more often occupy dilational strike-slip parts (including pull-aparts and transfer zones) and are less likely to occupy normal-fault step-overs.This is a relative regional setting statement, not a claim that pull-aparts or transfer zones dominate every valley or every hidden system. The paper describes a small mapped Walker Lane step-over sample and non-precise domain boundaries.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, sections 5–6.
N–NE faults are favorably oriented under WNW extension.Supported, with regional scope. The paper says N–NE faults are oriented approximately perpendicular to WNW-trending least principal stress / extension in the described study region and therefore are optimally oriented for slip/dilation relative to less favorably oriented fault sets.Do not hard-code one regional azimuth everywhere. Use local stress/strain evidence if available, and preserve uncertainty where catalogue dip or stress attributes are absent.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, implications; also Faulds et al. (2010), OSTI.
GeoDAWN footprint is Walker Lane-dominated.Supported as a broad description, not exact area fraction. Giddens & Faulds describe the GeoDAWN footprint as primarily encompassing the Walker Lane plus an east-trending arm into north-central Nevada.No precise footprint percentage is asserted here. Exact proportions require the actual official raster mask, which is absent.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, methods; USGS GeoDAWN release.
Gravity/magnetic edges, tilt, signed magnetic lows, and lidar scarps should score candidate corridors.Mechanistically motivated, not a source-validated predictor. Giddens & Faulds used regional gravity, magnetic, MT, lidar, and geological layers. They describe low magnetic / low resistivity anomalies associated with alteration as useful context and say geophysical data alone are insufficient.The exact transforms, multiscale fusion, weights, and ranking are this project's testable design choices; they have no measured DTI or AUC yet.Giddens & Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229, methods / limitations.

Project protocol versus published facts

  • Along-strike separation ≤3.5 × width: specified by the project prompt as a candidate-generation bound. The article's typical ramp aspect ratio motivates it, but the article does not prescribe this exact automated threshold.
  • Holdout alpha/beta/kernel, exact masking, AUC canary, duplicate thresholds, budget, and run-card fields: experiment protocol supplied in the project prompt; not geological findings from the paper.
  • “Why the named H33 file earned the public value cited in the prompt”: not established. The official leaderboard is participant-level and the owner page labels the named H33 file unscored. See the attribution audit.
