RANK 1 · TOP CANDIDATE

Domain-aware fault-tip graph with evidence-ranked corridors

Layer(s)
Catalogue polylines and their available dip/slip attributes; gravity anomaly and slope; reduced-to-pole / total magnetic intensity and horizontal/vertical derivative bands; the requested lidar-scarp channel; and the supplied strain/stress-orientation layer. Confirm every band and vector field from the actual stack before use. If the package lacks a regional stress orientation, the public USGS Quaternary-fault slip/dilation release is a candidate source to inspect—not a pre-approved feature: its stress values are attached to catalogue fault segments, so lineage, overlap, and fold-local leakage must be checked before use.
Physical signature
Construct a tip graph. For parallel normal faults with the same dip direction, retain both overlapping and underlapping tip pairs; use a ramp-width search interval W of 0.1–15 km and along-strike separation ≤3.5W. The width interval is a project search choice informed by the paper's 0.1–14.6 km measured range (mean 2.8 km); the 3.5W bound is a project candidate-generation rule, not a universal law. Generate a ramp corridor for each qualifying pair. Within it, score multi-scale horizontal-gradient and tilt coherence in gravity and magnetics, preserve the sign of magnetic lows, include scarp support, and weight orientation by consistency with the local extension field. Emit separate oblique-connector and en-échelon candidates; do not treat them as interchangeable geometries.
Why this could find a missing fault
The predicted targets are connector strands and related fractures inside a structurally plausible gap, not the already-mapped parent strands. Giddens & Faulds describe breached ramps with oblique connectors or en-échelon strands and report that hard linkage is common among higher-temperature step-overs. Their paper also warns that geophysical data work best with geological/fault data rather than alone.
Difference from this repository and prior examples
This repository had no model or code before this change, so the direct implementation difference is “new here.” The user's prior-result list includes catalogue-flank thinning and analog tip/step-over approaches; this candidate differs by generating corridors directly from visible catalogue tip-pair geometry and ranking corridor interiors with the stipulated multi-layer evidence. This is a proposal, not a claim that the prior websites were independently re-run or exhaustively audited.
Expected DTI improvement / cost
Highest qualitative prior of the five; no numeric lift is defensible before a holdout. High engineering cost because the graph, dip/kinematic handling, multiple transforms, fold-local reconstruction, corridor rationales, and registry tests must all be auditable.
Named non-fault mimic
A buried mafic dike or lithologic contact can make a coherent magnetic lineament. USGS reporting from Surprise Valley describes a long magnetic high interpreted as a buried dike and local anomaly breaks/bends associated with springs. Such evidence must not be called a fault without independent geometry/scarp support.

RANK 2

Walker Lane displacement-transfer corridors at dextral terminations

Layer(s)
Catalogue strike-slip fault tips, N–NE normal-fault arrays and their available sense-of-slip/dip data; gravity and magnetic derivatives/tilt; lidar-scarp channel; stress/strain orientation if available.
Physical signature
Identify a northwest-trending dextral structure that terminates into a distributed array of N–NNE normal faults. Place a broad transfer corridor on the receiving extensional array and its dilational intersections; seek convergent structural edges and scarp evidence, rather than simply extending the dextral trace.
Why this could find a missing fault
Transfer zones are defined by termination and distributed strain transfer, so their unrecorded splays can lie away from the mapped master strand. Giddens & Faulds specifically note that the Walker Lane differs from the other domains and that dilational strike-slip settings become relatively more important there.
Difference from prior work
Unlike a generic lineament detector or a normal-fault-only relay-ramp search, it requires a mapped dextral terminus plus a geometrically compatible normal-fault array. No such operator exists in this checkout.
Expected DTI improvement / cost
High qualitative prior within the Walker Lane; unknown and unmeasured elsewhere. High cost: kinematic classification and domain-specific analysis are required, and catalogue slip-sense gaps can make the candidate ineligible.
Named non-fault mimic
A basin margin or sedimentary/alluvial boundary may align with geophysical gradients and mimic the receiving edge of a transfer zone.

RANK 3

Kinematically verified releasing steps and pull-aparts

Layer(s)
Catalogue strike-slip segments with slip sense; step geometry; gravity lows and edge/tilt fields; magnetic lows and derivatives; lidar scarps.
Physical signature
Classify a step as releasing only after verifying the strike-slip sense and geometric handedness. For a dextral system, test right-stepping/releasing arrangements rather than assigning a “pull-apart” label from a line gap alone. Look for a basin-scale gravity low bounded by structural gradients, magnetic-low/edge evidence, and any scarped basin-bounding splays.
Why this could find a missing fault
The basin interior and margins may contain subsidiary strands that are not represented by the master-fault catalogue. The published regional literature describes pull-aparts as favorable transtensional settings, especially within or along the Walker Lane.
Difference from prior work
This is a slip-sense-conditioned corridor class; it is not a generic buffer around faults, nor a pixel-only low-anomaly detector. There is no pull-apart geometry implementation in this checkout.
Expected DTI improvement / cost
Medium–high qualitative prior, concentrated in the Walker Lane; unknown. Medium–high cost because slip sense, step polarity, basin geometry, and fold-local graph construction all need tests.
Named non-fault mimic
A sediment-filled basin can produce a gravity low and a sharp basin edge without an unmapped fault at the candidate pixels.

RANK 4

Signed magnetic-low and gravity-low corroboration inside structural corridors

Layer(s)
Reduced-to-pole and total magnetic intensity; horizontal/vertical magnetic derivatives; isostatic gravity and its slope; corridor geometry from the visible fault catalogue.
Physical signature
Retain signed low-anomaly information rather than taking absolute gradient magnitude only. Test whether an anomaly low, its flanking tilt/gradient edges, and a candidate structural corridor co-locate across more than one scale. Treat a low as corroborating evidence, not a direct fault label.
Why this could find a missing fault
The 2025 step-over study notes that basin fill can conceal faults and that geothermal upwelling may alter rocks and produce low magnetic / low-resistivity anomalies. These signals can reveal structure not in the fault catalogue, but the ambiguity is substantial.
Difference from prior work
It tests signed anomaly polarity specifically inside geometry-derived corridors. It does not replace the corridor prior with “dark pixels” or a global magnetic-low map. No signed-low feature or corridor gating is implemented here.
Expected DTI improvement / cost
Medium qualitative prior; unknown. Medium cost for signed multiscale transforms, normalization, and single-feature leakage canaries.
Named non-fault mimic
Unaltered lithologic contrasts and buried intrusive contacts can produce magnetic lows or highs without a fault.

RANK 5

Lidar-scarp continuity within predicted relay and transfer corridors

Layer(s)
The specified lidar-scarp channel or, only if permitted and present in the competition package, the official 1 m DEM inputs; corridor geometry; gravity/magnetic evidence used as cross-checks.
Physical signature
Use coherent, line-like scarp response that crosses or links the gap in the expected orientation. Assess continuity and profile shape across multiple directions/scales; down-weight isolated edges and features explained by drainage or built infrastructure.
Why this could find a missing fault
Subtle surface scarps may expose young or shallow strands that are absent from a generalized regional catalogue, while the corridor geometry supplies a prior where to look. A scarp is not required for blind faults, so absence is not evidence against deeper structure.
Difference from prior work
It is a corridor-conditioned use of the named scarp channel, not a global ridge/NMS raster or an unconditioned LiDAR score. The channel is not present in this checkout and has not been verified.
Expected DTI improvement / cost
Moderate qualitative prior; unknown. Low–medium cost if the precomputed channel is already cached; high or infeasible if it must be reconstructed from unavailable DEMs.
Named non-fault mimic
Fluvial terraces, fan scarps, landslide margins, road cuts, and irrigation/earthwork edges can resemble fault scarps.

Preregistered validation for rank one

  1. Use whole fault segments and spatial blocks; in each fold, buffer and hide the designated segments before any catalogue-derived feature is computed.
  2. Rebuild the tip graph and corridors from visible faults only. Pixel-mask visible faults exactly. Never let a withheld segment, its tip, or a feature derived from it enter the fold's proposal surface.
  3. Run each feature alone through the required leakage canary before fusion. Any AUC >0.90 is a leakage alarm until the feature lineage and fold-local derivation are demonstrated clean; do not use it in fusion before that review.
  4. Use the specified pooled distance-weighted Tversky evaluator (alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, triangular kernel 300 m) and compare corridor-on to corridor-off in paired spatial folds at matched prediction mass. Report evaluator version, withheld-positive count, pooled HOLDOUT-DTI and segment/block-aware 95% CI.
  5. Require a positive paired gate over both corridor-off and current best holdout baselines, a confidence interval that does not support a negative effect, clean leakage checks, and registry checks both before placement and on final dots: rank-correlation ≤0.90 to every prior raster and no more than 70% of dots within three pixels of any one prior. If a duplicate threshold is crossed, log it and stop. Then—and only then—consider a TIFF build. A holdout pass remains proxy evidence, not an organizer score.
