Why the reported H33 result is plausible — not proven

The value 0.2778 is USER-REPORTED, NOT ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED for this file. No submission-page receipt binding that value to the H33 hash is available here. The user gives conflicting leaderboard highs; the archived leaderboard snapshot is not a current feed.

The local family audit (evidence/e0_champion_ladder_measured.json) records progressively sparser dot fields. Under the implemented DTI, redundant dots can add false-positive mass without increasing the maximum coverage of a truth pixel. Thinning can therefore improve precision while retaining recovery within the distance kernel. This is a plausible explanation of the reported family trend, not a causal experiment on organizer hidden truth.

Removing catalogue-adjacent dots is not automatically a pure gain: an adjacent dot may cover an unmasked hidden fault. For a new unit dot with marginal truth coverage dT and self-credit m, the exact improvement condition is dT > 0.2 DTI (dT + 1 - m) for the stated weights. The simplified threshold m > 0.2 DTI only follows when dT=m. It is not a universal 5.6% fault-probability rule. There is no justified fixed score ceiling for sparse submissions.

Re-measured here (2026-10-20, from fetched registry bytes, evidence/relayfix_champion_anatomy.json): the H33 zeros file (sha256 c55bafc4…) holds 37,654 unit dots, none on the catalogue, nearest 2.24 px from it, median 1.96 km away, only 5.8% within 300 m of a mapped fault. At DTI 0.2778 a new dot pays only if its marginal kernel credit clears the metric bar (exact condition above; the 300 m kernel and α=0.2/β=0.8 were re-verified on the official problem page fetched this session). The reported ladder is therefore consistent with near-minimal mass on a wide, reasonably ranked field — dotted emission plus removal of below-bar catalogue-flank dots — not proof of uniquely correct geology.

Can a unique lane submission beat 0.2778? Not demonstrated. The public leaderboard fetched 2026-10-20 is led at 0.4413, so much better-ranked fields exist, but beating H33 by reusing its dots would fail this protocol's uniqueness rule, and our holdout is a screen (ρ≈0.51 cross-family), never a projection. This session's transfer-only candidate beat its same-evaluator holdout comparators; only an organizer receipt could convert that into a leaderboard claim, and no slot was spent.
