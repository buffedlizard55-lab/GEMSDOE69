Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED) — and can a unique lane raster beat it?

The file (GEMSDOE32, sha256 c55bafc4…, re-measured from bytes): 37,654 pixels equal to 1.0, zeros elsewhere; none on the catalogue (nearest 2.24 px), median 1.97 km from it, only 5.77% within 300 m of a mapped fault — a wide, sparse dot field.

How it got there (owner-reported ladder): h19-5 solid 121,131 px 0.1922 → d1.5 dotted 60,069 0.2477 → d2.8 44,090 0.2600 → h27-4-r1-solo 40,199 0.2708 → h33-2-b2 37,654 0.2778. Each step removed mass from one ranked field.

Why that works (official metric, model arithmetic): for a binary dot field, adding a dot with kernel credit k raises DTI only if k > 0.2·DTI (≈0.056 at 0.2778). Dotting keeps most coverage of a 1-D trace at a third of the mass; dots 100–200 m from mapped faults rarely earn credit because known faults are masked (forum 11516). So 0.2778 is near-minimal mass on a reasonably ranked wide field — not proof of correct geology.

Can this lane beat it? Not demonstrated. On the corrected holdout every corridor arm beats the no-prior control, but the best arm (transfer-only) duplicates a GEMSDOE53 raster, and this session's unique candidates (H69-M, H69-N) do not beat it. Holdout values (~0.03–0.05) are not comparable with 0.2778. Copying H33's dots would violate the uniqueness rule. Full write-up: docs/research/why-0278.md.
