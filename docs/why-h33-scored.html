Metric mechanism

Let T be weighted true-positive coverage, S total predicted mass, M the prediction-weighted nearest-truth kernel credit, and N the number of true pixels. With α=0.2 and β=0.8,

DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8N + ε].
A new unit dot improves DTI iff:
ΔT > 0.2 · DTI · (ΔT + 1 − m).

Here m is that dot's nearest-truth kernel credit and ΔT its new coverage after existing predictions. They need not be equal. Redundant nearby dots can add mass without much new coverage; thinning can therefore help, but it can also lose recall. The simplified m >0.2·DTI condition is only a special case, not a universal fault-probability threshold.

Geological interpretation and what would improve it

Sparse off-catalogue coverage is compatible with a good detector plus economical emission; it does not prove relay geology or geothermal permeability. Intrusive contacts, basin edges and shorelines are important confounders. A corridor prior must add independent, kinematically and physically coherent evidence rather than copy H33's dots or inherit its hidden-truth model as measured ground truth.
