PhD analysis: why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED-NOT-ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED), and what would beat it

1. The metric arithmetic

The competition metric is the distance-weighted Tversky index: DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + ε) with a 300 m triangular kernel (official problem page; transcribed in vendor/template66/gems/metric.py). False negatives cost 4× as much as false positives. Adding one dot with hit probability p near previously uncovered truth raises DTI iff p > α·DTI; at DTI = 0.2778 the break-even is p > 0.0556. Any dot better than ~5.6% likely to touch a hidden fault within 300 m is score-positive. Sparse, single-basin submissions (1–2k dots) therefore cap near 0.02–0.10: they pay the FN penalty on the other 99% of the basin.

2. What h33-2-b2 actually is (GEMSDOE32 audit bytes)

  • 37,654 unit dots (value 1.0), basin-wide, on the d28/h19-5 multi-physics lineament propensity (“0.2708 base”), zeros mode, all-finite [0,1] — format-safe.
  • Catalogue flank prune B = 2 px (200 m): every dot within 200 m of a mapped fault deleted. Known faults are masked out of scoring, so such dots earn zero TP credit while risking FP mass; excising them is a pure gain (audit: 0 dots within 100 m or 200 m of the catalogue after the prune).
  • Live-mirror instrument: +0.00487 DTI in 4/4 folds vs the pre-prune base (HOLDOUT-DTI class in the GEMSDOE32 audit; the live receipt value quoted on that site is 0.2778).

3. Why it beat the other 37k-dot submissions

Within the family, three decisions separate 0.26–0.2708 from 0.2778: (a) dot spacing calibrated near the 300 m kernel (the d2.8-px thinning beat d1.5 at equal physics: 0.2600 vs 0.2477 on receipts quoted in the prompt); (b) basin-wide coverage at the marginal bar above, rather than concentrated bets; (c) the 200 m flank prune, worth the residual +0.007. The emission's shape — dots on multi-physics lineaments, off catalogue — matches the DTI optimum for a field of thin hidden faults.

4. Can we score higher than 0.2778? — the honest answer

Mechanism, yes: the marginal-bar argument says any propensity improvement that puts more dots above the p > 0.2·DTI line raises the score; corridor geometry is exactly such an improvement if hidden faults preferentially occupy step-over corridors (the physical claim this lane tests). Calibration, honestly uncertain: the family's own instrument ranked live scores with cross-family Spearman ρ≈0.51 (p≈0.09) only (GEMSDOE32 instrument-calibration evidence), and the user-cited leaderboard highs (0.3774, 0.3195 — USER-REPORTED) sit +0.04–+0.10 above the family ceiling, implying signal the family instrument has not captured. This lane therefore: validate on the spatially-blocked holdout, pass the gate, publish the TIF with an explicit OK/DO-NOT-SUBMIT banner, and treat promotion to a scored slot as a separate selector decision.
