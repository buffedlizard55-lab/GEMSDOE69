## A plausible metric mechanism — not a causal explanation

The repository's source register records the competition metric as distance-weighted Tversky with alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, and a 300 m triangular kernel (official problem-page reference; not re-fetched in this review). Under the metric implementation, reducing redundant prediction mass can help when removed predictions add false-positive cost but little new kernel-weighted coverage. A binary sparse field may therefore be more efficient than a broad ribbon **if** it retains credit near hidden faults.

Conditionally taking 0.2778 as the DTI input, the metric's single-dot marginal-credit identity gives a MODEL-DERIVED break-even credit of about 0.056 (alpha × DTI). This is algebra conditional on an unconfirmed score, not a probability that any particular pixel contains a hidden fault, and not an estimate of the hidden-fault distribution. The measured file geometry plus owner-reported ladder is consistent with a sparse-mass/precision mechanism, but cannot establish why the organizer assigned the reported value.

In particular, the repository has no hidden truth with which to establish that new faults “rarely” occur near mapped traces. A pruning effect can be a plausible metric explanation without proving that geological claim. Nor does good fault-map alignment establish direct geothermal vent discovery: the competition target is hidden faults as a proxy, not vent labels.
