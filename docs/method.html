Method lane and its limits

Candidate corridors are proposed from the mapped catalogue tip graph. Geometry restrictions include spacing W of 0.1–15 km and along-strike separation no greater than 3.5W. The raster catalogue does not encode dip polarity or slip sense; parallel strike is not a measurement of same dip, and dextral/releasing classification remains a stated regional prior.

The shared project implementation renders oblique connectors and en-échelon/pull-apart geometries; the current candidate restricts its scored proposals to transfer-overlap pairs. Physics scores use gravity/magnetic horizontal gradients and tilt-derived fields, magnetic lows, a LiDAR scarp magnitude composite, and a fixed orientation prior. Dots are selected by the pinned shared template allocator. No private template fork was created for this review.

What the joint scorer actually computes

src/gems69/scoring_joint.py takes maxima across gravity and magnetic feature groups, combines them with min(E_grav, E_mag), and adds scalar magnetic-low, LiDAR, orientation, and geometry terms. It does not compute signed edge polarity, spatial co-location, phase alignment, or cross-height persistence. This distinction is why the new hypothesis register does not call the earlier H69-J run a spatial phase test.

Current experiment

H69-H2 transfer-only: oblique-connector corridors at overlapping transfer steps, selected from the repaired catalogue tip graph.

Mechanism: Tip-pair geometry proposes transfer-overlap corridors; existing gravity/magnetic HG and tilt, magnetic lows, lidar scarp, and N-NE orientation score them; the shared greedy allocator places dots. Dip polarity and dextral slip sense are not observed in the raster catalogue.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithologic contacts, restraining-overlap pressure ridges, pluvial shorelines, and road-cut scarps can mimic the geophysical or lidar lineaments.
