DrivenData organizer
problem		read
  • The challenge predicts geological faults indicative of geothermal resources; it does not directly label geothermal vents.
  • New expert-labeled faults absent from the current public database form the initial test set. The final round expands labels by expert review.
  • The distance-weighted Tversky index uses alpha=0.2, beta=0.8 and a triangular distance kernel with support R=300 m.
  • Predictions are pixel-wise confidence/probability values in [0,1], single-layer float32 GeoTIFF, EPSG:32611, 100 m, matching training bounds; outside-bounds data is null or NaN.
  • The sample submission is a template. Labels are offered in both vector and raster form.
  • External data requires a license permitting competition use and sharing with the sponsor.
Does not authenticate this repository's mirrored sample, define our local literal registry policy, reveal test labels, or diagnose a rejected user upload.

    Stanford Geothermal Workshop; Giddens and Faulds (2025), SGP-TR-229
    giddens2025    		read
    • More than 450 step-overs were identified; reported relay widths are 0.1-14.6 km, mean 2.8 km.
    • Section 3 defines a normal-fault step-over as transfer to an adjacent parallel fault with the same direction of dip; relay structures are typically 3-3.5 times longer than wide.
    • About 70% of higher-temperature (>120 C) step-overs are hard-linked; the abstract reports about 74% between north to north-northeast strands.
    • Breaching structures range from oblique-slip connectors to en echelon strands parallel to major faults.
    • North-to-northeast faults are favorably oriented relative to WNW extension, but regional strain and fault attributes vary.
    • Range-front mapping bias, uncertain tectonic-domain boundaries, limited detailed mapping and incomplete lidar coverage are explicit limitations.
    These population associations are not probabilities calibrated to the competition's hidden faults. Same strike does not establish same dip. The 3.5W acceptance cutoff is the user's modeling rule, not an empirically validated classifier boundary.
    Stanford Geothermal Workshop; Faulds and colleagues (2026)
    faulds2026    		read
    • Fault terminations, intersections, relay ramps, accommodation zones, pull-aparts and displacement-transfer zones are favorable structural settings.
    • Pull-aparts and displacement-transfer zones are more prominent within and near the transtensional Walker Lane.
    • Minor faults with little recent rupture can be obscured by lake sediments and shoreline features; gravity data helps delineate basin structures.
    No spatial overlap or geothermal target in our candidate was newly verified from this paper. The paper's polygons must not be used as holdout labels or features without ancestry/leakage checks.
